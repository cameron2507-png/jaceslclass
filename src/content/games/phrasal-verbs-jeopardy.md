A 5×5 Jeopardy board built around 25 high-frequency phrasal verbs at B1/B2 level. Each clue has a clear definition, the answer in capitals, and a real-world example sentence to anchor the meaning.

## Categories

- **Work & Business** — carry out, put off, hand in, come up with, look into
- **Relationships** — break up, get along, run into, stand by, look up to
- **Travel & Movement** — take off, check in, drop by, turn up, move on
- **Daily Life** — get up, try on, come across, give up, tidy up
- **Emotions** — cheer up, blow up, get over, look forward to, bottle up

## Why these phrasal verbs

These are the phrasal verbs French learners genuinely encounter — in films, in workplaces, in everyday conversation. The list deliberately mixes separable and inseparable verbs and includes a few three-word phrasal verbs (look up to, look forward to, come up with) at the higher values, since these are the ones intermediate learners typically avoid in production.

## Lesson ideas

- **End-of-unit review** after a phrasal verbs lesson — gives students a chance to recycle vocabulary in a low-stakes way.
- **Warmer for an upper-intermediate group** — even if some students don't know every verb, the game format keeps everyone engaged through the discussion phase.
- **Pre-exam practice** — phrasal verbs appear regularly in BULATS, TOEIC, Cambridge B1/B2, and DELF/DALF exams.
- **Mixed-ability classes** — let stronger students choose 400 and 500 values, weaker students 100 and 200.

## Variations

- **Sentence variation**: after a team gets the answer right, they earn double points if they can also produce a new sentence using the phrasal verb.
- **No reveals**: hide the example and ask students to make up their own sentence as part of the answer.
- **Wager round**: at the end of the game, allow each team to wager up to half their score on a final bonus phrasal verb of your choice.

## Skills covered

Listening, reading, vocabulary recall, contextual inference, paraphrasing under pressure.
